# DPIA screening — Lifeline Daily health tools (Stage 1)

Drafted by the build agent for the privacy reviewer (NFR1-01, DEC-21, DEC-36); approved at HG-03. The nine screening questions follow the ICO's DPIA screening checklist (the EDPB/WP248 high-risk criteria the ICO adopts). The data-flow inventory is `privacy/data-flow.json` (SCH-12).

**Processing in scope.** Two calculators (Daily Protein Target, Diabetes Risk Check) run in the visitor's browser. Nothing entered is sent to Lifeline Daily unless the visitor asks for the result by e-mail and ticks the consent box. Then the endpoint sends one e-mail through the site's mail provider (an SMTP processor, which holds the message under its own retention terms) and keeps no copy. This rests on DEC-46: the production mail settings are assumed to be `transport = smtp` with an empty `bcc_owner`, pending verification. If verification finds the `log` transport (which writes every message to an outbox on the server) or an owner BCC, health e-mails would be stored, DEC-21 reopens and this screening must be redone. Endpoint logs hold only timestamp, tool, outcome code and a daily-salted IP hash, for 30 days (DEC-20). The rate limiter keeps a small state file per tool and address, named with an unsalted MD5 of the IP, holding recent request times; old times are pruned but the file is not deleted (finding, DEC-87 proposal: salted hash and scheduled purge). Analytics is cookieless Plausible counting the SCH-09 tool events with props `tool` and `source` only, never form values (DEC-11, FR1-16); Plausible itself keeps a daily-rotated salted hash of IP and user agent to count unique visitors, which is processor-held pseudonymous data that our code never creates or receives.

| # | Screening question | Answer | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Evaluation or scoring, including profiling and predicting? | Yes — limited | The diabetes tool estimates a 10-year risk and the protein tool a daily target. The estimate is shown to the visitor only; Lifeline Daily keeps no profile and makes no prediction about anyone. |
| 2 | Automated decision-making with legal or similarly significant effect? | No | The result is educational screening information. No decision about the visitor is taken, and the visitor is told to speak to a clinician. |
| 3 | Systematic monitoring of individuals? | No | No accounts, tracking cookies or cross-site identifiers. Analytics is aggregate and cookieless; the only per-visitor value is Plausible's daily-rotated hash, held by the processor. The rate-limit files and the daily-salted log hash exist for abuse prevention and are not used to follow anyone. |
| 4 | Special category or highly personal data? | Yes | Health inputs and results (Art. 9), including the protein guideline sources that applied (they reveal pregnancy, illness, age or diet). They stay in the browser unless the visitor gives explicit consent to e-mail the result (DEC-21); they are never logged, stored or sent to analytics. |
| 5 | Processing on a large scale? | No | A small editorial site; no database of results is created. |
| 6 | Matching or combining datasets? | No | No linking of tool data with any other dataset; analytics events carry no form value and cannot be joined to an e-mail. |
| 7 | Data concerning vulnerable data subjects? | Possibly | Visitors may be unwell or anxious about their health. Mitigations: emergency and symptom banners take priority over any number, urgent-care guidance (DEC-37), plain-language copy reviewed at HG-02, and a protein tool limited to adults. |
| 8 | Innovative use or new technological or organisational solutions? | No | Published, established calculation methods run client-side; no AI or novel technique. |
| 9 | Processing that prevents data subjects exercising a right or using a service? | No | The tools are free and work without an e-mail address or any account; nothing is stored that a right would need to reach. |

**Outcome.** Criteria 1 and 4 are met in part, and 7 possibly. WP248 says processing that meets two or more criteria will in most cases need a DPIA, and the controller must justify a decision not to carry one out. That justification is: (a) the evaluation in criterion 1 never leaves the visitor's device and produces no profile, decision or record about them; (b) the special-category data in criterion 4 leaves the device only in one e-mail the visitor asks for with explicit consent, which our code does not store (subject to DEC-46 being verified); (c) the possible vulnerability in criterion 7 is mitigated by the safety design above. Taken together, no high-risk processing of the kind WP248 targets (systematic, large-scale, profiling or combining) remains. Screening recommendation for the privacy reviewer: a full DPIA is not required for Stage 1, on two conditions. DEC-46 must be verified before release: if it fails, a full DPIA is required. DEC-87 should be decided: rate-limit files with a salted hash and a scheduled purge. Record this screening, the data-flow inventory and the Privacy Policy update (DEC-21) at HG-03, and re-screen if any result is ever stored, analytics ever carries form values, or a new processor is added.

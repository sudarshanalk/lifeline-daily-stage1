What this means

Both measures point the same way: weight is above the range where risk of type 2 diabetes and heart disease is lowest for your background, and some of it sits around the middle. BMI cannot tell muscle from fat, which is why the waist measure is shown alongside it.

A modest change matters more than a target number. Losing 3–5 % of body weight, roughly 2–3 kg for you, measurably lowers risk.

How this was calculated
BMI = weight ÷ height². Healthy ranges follow WHO (2004) and NICE (2023) guidance, which set lower cut-offs for South Asian, Chinese and other Asian backgrounds (23 and 27.5 instead of 25 and 30).
